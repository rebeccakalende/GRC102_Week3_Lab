# Task 1: Governance Architecture and Stakeholder Map

## 1.1 Governance Gap Assessment

TechGlobal is a fast-growing technology organisation with approximately 2,500 employees across five global offices. Its security governance model has not developed at the same pace as its business growth.

The current model is informal and IT-centric, with most security decisions concentrated in the IT Director. This creates several governance weaknesses.

### Identified Governance Gaps

| No. | Governance Weakness | Business or Risk Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Security decisions are concentrated with the IT Director. | Creates excessive dependence on one person and increases the risk of insufficient independent review. |
| 2 | There is no formal Security Governance Committee. | Security, risk, finance, legal, and business priorities may not be coordinated effectively. |
| 3 | Business units make inconsistent security decisions. | Creates uneven security controls across the five global offices. |
| 4 | The CEO and Board have limited visibility into cyber risk. | Senior leadership may not understand major cyber threats, financial exposure, or decisions requiring oversight. |
| 5 | There are no reliable escalation paths. | Significant cyber incidents may not reach executive management or the Board quickly enough. |
| 6 | The IT Director acts as security owner, risk approver, and operational decision-maker. | Creates a segregation-of-duties concern because one person controls several stages of the decision process. |

### Consultant Justification

TechGlobal’s current governance model is informal and IT-centric. Although this approach may have been suitable when the organisation was smaller, its growth to approximately 2,500 employees across five global offices requires a more structured and cross-functional governance model.

The proposed governance structure should separate security oversight from technical implementation, establish formal governance committees, clarify decision-making authority, and ensure that significant cyber risks are escalated to executive management and the Board.

This approach will improve accountability, strengthen risk oversight, support consistent security practices across business units, and align cybersecurity decisions with TechGlobal’s business objectives.
